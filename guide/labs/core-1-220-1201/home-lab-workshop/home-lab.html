    YOUR VIRTUAL MACHINES

    3 · Operate and test each guest

    Independent educational simulation aligned to CompTIA A+ 220-1201. No real hardware, operating systems, packets, subscriptions or cloud resources are changed. Settings and timing are simplified and documented in the Lab manual. Rack placement and VLAN practice extend the A+ foundations.

    Your lab manual

    Narration: Press Play lesson. Use Pause/Resume or Stop. Voice & listening options lets you choose an available English voice, speed, and optional automatic reading of each next lesson. Device explanations and test results have separate read buttons. No sound starts on page load.

    First visit: Start with Home network from zero. Read each prompt, open its exact steps, perform the action, then choose Check & continue. The lesson checks the live configuration.

    1. Place: Drag an equipment handle from the shelf onto the workbench, or click to add it. Drag placed equipment to rearrange it; its cables follow. Press Escape to cancel a drag. Focus a placed device and use arrow keys to move it (Shift moves farther). Click its picture to open its operating panel. Choose a cable, click the first socket, then click a matching socket on the other device. Click a seated plug to unplug it. Power buttons, PoE switches, link lights, and laptop apps respond to the lab state. Advanced settings opens detailed configuration. To return equipment, drag it onto the equipment shelf or the Return to shelf drop zone. You can also select it and click Return to equipment shelf. This removes attached cables and hosted VMs from the build; Undo restores the item and any connections whose ports are still available. Rack mounting uses the checked rails/unit controls.
    2. Prepare: Ground the ESD mat and wrist strap before fitting internal parts. Shut down and unplug equipment first.
    3. Connect: In a device’s Ports section, choose a source port, destination and destination port, cable and length. Matching plugs, free ports and length limits are checked.
    4. Configure: Supply power and turn each device on. Set DHCP, addresses, services, RAID and firmware in the appropriate inspector.
    5. Test: Choose source, destination and test. Read the first failure and follow the path. Ping proves a different thing from HTTPS or file sharing.
    6. Experiment: Break a cable, fail a drive, change a gateway, isolate a switch port, or remove wall power. Watch the consequences.

    Model boundaries

    Clients use IPv4. The guided DHCP pool uses /24 networks. Switch access ports separate broadcast groups; this version does not simulate tagged trunks, STP, dynamic routing, IPv6 or radio propagation. The two-subnet exercise uses two physical router interfaces. Router WAN, Internet DNS and remote service responses are modeled.

    UPS batteries start charged and support a brief outage; runtime and battery aging are not simulated. Power limits are component ratings within this lab, not electrical-installation guidance. The teaching wall supply is assigned a conservative 1,800 W ceiling; real continuous-load and local-code limits require separate planning.

    VMs model resource allocation, storage dependencies and virtual-switch isolation. They do not execute a guest kernel. OS installation and RAID initialization are teaching actions with shortened time. Rebuild assumes healthy remaining blocks; failures beyond tolerance require reset or a recovery plan. External guests receive their own modeled DHCP leases. Internal and private switch choices demonstrate isolation; guest-to-guest application traffic is outside this version. Cloud provisioning and backup are simulations; no data leaves this page.

    The scene art is illustrative. Labeled port controls and rule checks define the modeled hardware. Workbench positions are presentation choices; rack units and collisions are checked. 1U is 1.75 inches. All cable lengths are in feet.

    Power and virtual-switch references

    • Schneider Electric: surge strips and UPS connections
    • Philips: connect surge protectors directly to a grounded outlet
    • Microsoft: external, internal and private virtual switches

    The surge protector has six outlets, a simulated 1,800 W total ceiling, and no battery. Its incoming cord is represented by the POWER IN teaching socket. Voltage spikes, protection wear and grounding faults are not simulated. The lab blocks chaining a surge protector with another distribution device. The UPS-to-PDU teaching path assumes a compatible manufacturer-approved rack PDU.
